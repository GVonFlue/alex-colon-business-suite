1. SPEED TO LEAD, AND STAGE TIMINGS   src/lib/velocity.js  (new)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

His words: "I especially want first-response time tracked because
speed-to-lead matters." None of it existed.

Pure functions, no React and no database, so every screen that ever shows a
timing gets the same answer from the same place. Two screens computing "days
to close" two different ways is how a CRM starts disagreeing with itself.

NOTHING NEW IS RECORDED. Every contact already carries created_at and an
activity array with an `at` and a `kind`, so these are derivable for leads
that already exist. No migration, no backfill.

THE ONE DECISION EVERYTHING RESTS ON: a NOTE is not a response.
api/lead-intake.js writes a note the instant a website lead lands. If notes
counted, every website lead would read as answered in zero seconds and the
metric would be measuring how fast the webhook fired rather than how fast Alex
called. Only a call, text, email or meeting counts.

MEDIAN, NOT MEAN. One lead that sat over a holiday drags a mean far enough to
make a good month look bad. Median of 1, 3 and 11 hours is 3; the mean is 5.

NULL IS NOT ZERO. "Answered instantly" and "never answered" are opposite
facts. Null renders as an em dash, and humanHours(0) returns "1 min" rather
than "0" so it can never be misread as instant.

2. THE DASHBOARD SECTION   "Speed to lead & lost reasons"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Left half is the working one: leads still waiting on a first attempt, longest
first, red past 24 hours. A median over last quarter is a report; three leads
sitting since Tuesday is something to do before lunch. The median sits small
beside it for context.

Right half is where leads fall out, grouped by the reason recorded at the
time. Contract-to-close, closed deals only, underneath.

It registers in DASH_SECTIONS, and reconcileOrder() inserts a new key at its
canonical position rather than appending -- so it appears in the right place
on an install that already has a saved dashboard order.

3. LOST REASONS, AND MOTIVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

His eight reasons verbatim, plus Other. The field appears on the contact ONLY
when the stage is one the settings mark as lost -- not on a hardcoded 'lost'
key, so a renamed or added lost stage still collects it.

NOT enforced as a blocking validation, deliberately. A required field on the
way out of a record somebody is trying to close teaches them to pick the first
option in the list, and a wrong reason looks like data in a way a blank does
not. Blanks are counted and reported SEPARATELY from "Other" for the same
reason: "Other" is a reason somebody chose, blank is one nobody recorded.

MOTIVATION was the one field on his list that existed nowhere. Ten options,
editable in Settings. The intake leaves it blank rather than guessing from
which form they filled in -- Alex fills it on the first call, which is when he
finds out.

Both lists are editable in Settings under "Lead sources and picklists".

4. NEW LEADS NOW EMAIL HIM   api/lead-intake.js
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

THIS IS THE ONE WITH CONSEQUENCES TODAY. The intake stored the contact and
stopped. The email Alex actually receives comes from the Apps Script on the
WEBSITE'S sheet webhook -- so if that path ever changed, his notifications
would go silent and the CRM would not have covered for it.

Not routed through api/notify.js: that handler requires a Supabase session,
which a server-to-server webhook does not have and should not be given.

Not awaited. The website gives this endpoint eight seconds and never retries,
so a slow mail provider must never be why a lead fails to store. The row is
saved before the email fires.

TO TURN IT ON, set three env vars on the CRM project:
    RESEND_API_KEY      a Resend key
    NOTIFY_FROM         a verified sender, e.g. leads@getproytech.com
    NOTIFY_TO           Alex@athomewichita.com
Unconfigured it logs one line and returns. The lead still lands in the CRM,
the sheet and the Apps Script email either way, so this is safe to upload
before the keys exist.

WHAT I DID NOT BUILD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

COST PER LEAD / APPOINTMENT / CLOSING, and campaign-level attribution. It
needs marketing spend tied to sources and a campaign field on the contact, and
it is the largest of the four. Worth noting the CRM ALREADY has source ROI by
closed GCI on the dashboard -- what is missing is the cost side, not the whole
report.

HIS 11 STAGES vs THE CRM'S 9. His list has "Attempting Contact" separate from
Contacted, a "Closed" stage, and "Sphere/Past Client". The CRM merges the
first two, treats under-contract as won, and handles past clients as a SOURCE
rather than a stage. I would leave it -- his eleven read like a generic CRM
lifecycle, the nine are side-aware and match how a deal actually moves -- but
it is his business, so show him rather than deciding for him.
